# Acme support review: July to September

## Volume

Acme's support team handled 18,420 tickets in the third quarter, up
from 15,960 in the second quarter, an increase of 15 percent. The
rise came almost entirely from August, when the monsoon sale doubled
order volume for two weeks. July and September were close to the
previous quarter's levels, with 5,610 and 5,880 tickets respectively.

Email remained the largest channel with 52 percent of tickets. Chat
grew from 31 to 38 percent after the chat widget moved to the order
page in July. Phone fell to 10 percent; the phone line now closes at
8 pm instead of 10 pm, and the change produced no measurable rise in
complaints.

## Response times

The median first response time was 3 hours 40 minutes, against a
target of 4 hours. During the sale the median rose to 9 hours, and
1,240 tickets waited more than 24 hours for a first reply. Customers
who waited more than a day rated their experience 2.1 out of 5 on
average, compared with 4.3 for those answered within four hours.

The median time to resolution was 26 hours. Refund requests were the
slowest category, at a median of 61 hours, because every refund above
INR 1,000 needed a supervisor and only two supervisors work per
shift.

## Categories

Delivery questions were the largest category at 41 percent of
tickets, most of them asking where an order was. Returns and refunds
made up 24 percent, product questions 18 percent, payment problems 9
percent and account issues 8 percent.

Payment problems doubled in the second half of August. The cause was
a change at the payment provider that declined some international
cards; it was fixed on 29 August. About 600 tickets were linked to it.

## The pilot assistant

In September, the support assistant described in the engineering
plan answered order-status questions in chat for 20 percent of
customers. It resolved 71 percent of those conversations without a
person, mostly by looking up delivery status. Customers rated
assistant conversations 4.1 out of 5, slightly below the 4.3 for
human agents.

The assistant handed over 29 percent of conversations. The most
common reasons were refund requests, which it is not allowed to
complete, and questions about orders placed under a different email
address. In 14 conversations it gave a delivery date that was not in
the order data; all 14 were caught in the weekly review, and the
prompt was changed on 18 September to forbid estimated dates.

## Staffing

The team had 46 agents at the start of the quarter and 52 at the
end. Six temporary agents were hired for the sale and three of them
stayed. Attrition was 4 percent, the lowest this year. Training a new
agent still takes three weeks before they work alone.

## Recommendations

First, add a third supervisor per shift during sales, or raise the
refund approval threshold for customers with a good history, to cut
refund resolution time. Second, extend the pilot assistant to 50
percent of chat customers in October, keeping the weekly review of
its answers. Third, ask the payment team to alert support within an
hour of any provider change, so that agents can answer with facts
instead of guesses.
